# Camera B — first system study

Camera B and the chalk treatment were accepted by the user. This study uses the
nearest end dwelling and keeps the same camera azimuth, with a steeper pitch to see rooms.

## What is shown

- Three separated source levels, with presentation-only gaps of 3 coordinate units.
- Lowered architectural walls and shower screens to expose rooms and fixtures.
- Thirteen source fixture assemblies: four at ground level, three at the living
  level and six at the bedroom level.
- A single illustrative waste/riser arrangement, coloured dark mineral grey.
- An amber ring at the living-level floor crossing, linked to example decision HYD-01.
- Dashed registration marks between separated riser segments. These are not pipework.

The roof remains in its own editable collection but is hidden in these close-ups.
The full source development is retained in hidden collections. No source GLB edits.

## Two presentation states

`05-hydraulic-cutaway-b.png` keeps chalk architecture to explain the rooms and floors.
`06-hydraulic-focus-b.png` uses translucent architecture to reveal the service routes.
The editable Blender file opens in the latter state; source fixture references and
route coordinates are stored in `hydraulic-study.json`.

## What HYD-01 means

**Question:** Where should the riser pass through the floor, and who owns the
penetration detail?

**People involved:** architect, hydraulic consultant and structural consultant.

**Illustrative outcome to show in the animation:** an agreed route and reserved
zone are recorded, with penetration responsibility carried into consultant and
trade scope. The amber marker becomes neutral only after that decision is shown.

This is not a detected clash or a validated hydraulic design. Branch connections
are derived from fixture bounds for illustration; exact outlets, pipe sizes,
gradients, ventilation, capacity, support and penetration details are not designed.
The fixture counts and level grouping describe the imported model, not a verified
quantity survey. The assembly gaps and cutaway geometry are display conventions.
