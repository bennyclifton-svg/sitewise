# Advisory Services Guide

## Purpose and professional boundary

Use this overlay when the commissioned outcome is advice rather than delivery
of physical work. Typical commissions include technical due diligence,
feasibility, condition assessment, capital expenditure planning, design review,
project-definition advice and procurement support.

An advisory artefact does not become a design, approval, certification,
valuation, legal opinion or warranty merely because it is detailed. Define the
professional purpose, intended users, reliance, competence and limitations.
Refer regulated, legal, financial, valuation, environmental and safety
conclusions to appropriately qualified advisers.

## Define the decision before the scope

Record:

- the client decision the advice must support;
- the asset, transaction, development option or design being assessed;
- intended users and permitted reliance;
- reporting date and decision deadline;
- materiality thresholds and priority criteria;
- information expected from the client or third parties;
- access, survey, testing and stakeholder constraints;
- required level of cost, programme and risk confidence;
- exclusions and matters to be referred elsewhere.

Avoid generic objectives such as "review the project." A useful objective names
the decision, the criteria and the evidence needed.

## Advisory service types

### Technical due diligence

Define the transaction or investment context, asset boundary, tenure and
available data room. Address physical condition, approvals, compliance records,
services capacity, maintenance history, material defects, planned works,
operational constraints and further investigations. Separate document findings,
site observations and unverified representations.

### Feasibility and option analysis

Confirm the brief, constraints, planning pathway, functional options, enabling
works, utilities, procurement route, programme dependencies, risk allowances
and decision gates. Comparisons should use consistent scope and assumptions.
State when technical uncertainty prevents a meaningful option ranking.

### Condition and capital planning

Define the asset hierarchy, inspection coverage and condition scale. Record
defects, consequence, recommended action, timing basis, dependencies and
evidence. Capital forecasts should identify source quantities, inclusions,
exclusions, basis date, escalation treatment, uncertainty and overlap with
routine maintenance. Do not present modelled expenditure as a quotation.

### Design review

Define the design stage, review criteria, disciplines, interfaces and expected
response process. Comments should cite the reviewed document and location,
describe the issue and consequence, identify the responsible responder and
state whether the comment is open or closed. A peer review does not transfer
design responsibility from the designer of record.

### Procurement support

Define the package, procurement stage, probity rules and extent of assistance.
Keep compliance assessment, clarification, risk review and recommendation
traceable to the issued request and addenda. Commercial arithmetic should be
performed deterministically from the returned data, not generated from prose.

## Evidence plan

Create an information request aligned to the decision. For each item record:

- requested document or data;
- owner and due date;
- received revision and issue purpose;
- completeness and reliability;
- effect of absence on findings;
- follow-up action or verification method.

Project evidence outranks platform guidance. Interviews and verbal statements
should identify speaker, date and verification status. If access is
representative rather than complete, describe the sampled areas and do not
extrapolate silently.

## Inspection, survey and testing

Before site work, define access permissions, inductions, safety controls,
occupied-area protocols, destructive limitations, shutdowns and reinstatement.
The methodology should distinguish:

- visual inspection;
- measured survey;
- non-destructive testing;
- intrusive opening-up;
- laboratory or specialist testing;
- desktop inference requiring later verification.

Record inaccessible areas and environmental conditions that may affect
observations. Photographs should be indexed to location and finding.

## Analysis and confidence

Every material finding should show:

- the question or criterion;
- evidence reviewed;
- observation or calculation;
- conclusion;
- consequence for the client's decision;
- recommended action, owner and timing;
- confidence and any verification dependency.

Use a consistent confidence scale defined in the report. High confidence should
require direct, current and relevant evidence. Repetition across weak sources
does not create strong evidence.

Cost, quantity, percentage, scoring and comparison calculations must be
deterministic and reviewable. Label estimates by maturity and source; do not
invent market rates. Programme advice should expose approvals, information,
access and third-party dependencies rather than give an unsupported single
date.

## Report and register discipline

Structure deliverables so the decision-maker can trace:

- executive conclusion and recommended next action;
- scope, purpose, reliance and limitations;
- evidence and methodology;
- findings by asset, option, discipline or criterion;
- risk, action and information-gap registers;
- cost and programme basis where included;
- supporting photographs, schedules and calculations;
- unresolved matters and escalation routes.

Keep observations separate from recommendations. Avoid treating absence of a
record as proof of non-compliance, or the presence of a certificate as proof of
current physical condition.

## Consultant RFP minimum returnables

An advisory RFP should request:

- understanding of the decision and proposed methodology;
- deliverables, registers and presentation format;
- evidence request and information cutoff;
- inspection coverage, samples, testing and access assumptions;
- materiality, prioritisation and confidence method;
- programme, workshops and draft-review cycle;
- reliance, liability, intellectual property and report-use assumptions;
- proposed team, competence, registrations and conflicts;
- fee by stage or deliverable, reimbursables and variation triggers;
- exclusions, dependencies and recommended optional investigations.

Where multiple disciplines contribute, nominate a lead responsible for
integrating interfaces, removing duplicate findings and producing one coherent
decision set.

## PMP contribution

For an advisory commission, the PMP should state the client decision, intended
users, scope boundary, evidence plan, access method, consultant responsibilities,
analysis criteria, deliverables, review gates, calculation controls, reliance,
programme, communications, change control and the route for urgent findings.

Advisory programmes should include information receipt, fieldwork, specialist
testing, factual verification, draft review and final issue. Do not schedule
only a final report date.

## Common failures and low-confidence flags

Common failures are an undefined decision, broad scope with no materiality
threshold, undocumented reliance, incomplete data-room review, inaccessible
areas omitted from limitations, visual observations presented as diagnosis,
cost figures without source or basis, recommendations outside competence,
duplicate multidisciplinary findings and no owner for next actions.

Flag low confidence when the client decision, asset boundary, evidence cutoff,
access coverage, design issue, comparison basis, cost source, intended reliance
or responsible specialist is not established. Say what would raise confidence.
