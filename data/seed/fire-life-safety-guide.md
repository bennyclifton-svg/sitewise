# Fire and Life-Safety Guide

## Purpose and professional boundary

Use this guide to manage the fire and life-safety workstream across planning,
design, procurement, construction, commissioning and occupation.

This guide does not classify a building, determine a deemed-to-satisfy or
performance pathway, design a fire-safety measure, approve a performance
solution, certify work or give emergency advice. Project conclusions must come
from current evidence and the responsible fire engineer, architect, services
designers, certifier, competent fire-safety practitioners, authority and
emergency-planning personnel.

Verify current NCC and NSW requirements, adopted standards, planning and
approval conditions, existing fire-safety schedules and authority advice.

## Activation

Activate when the task involves:

- building classification, fire compartments, construction type or egress;
- a fire-engineered performance solution or fire-safety strategy;
- sprinklers, hydrants, hose reels, detection, alarm or occupant warning;
- smoke control, stair pressurisation, shutdowns or cause and effect;
- passive fire resistance, penetrations, joints, doors or dampers;
- emergency lighting, exit signs, emergency power or firefighter facilities;
- essential or fire-safety measures, testing, certification or handover;
- an existing-building upgrade, impairment or fire-order response.

Select the relevant modules. Do not infer that every listed system applies.

## Evidence sweep

Seek:

- current classification, use, population and operating assumptions;
- approved plans, specifications and building approvals;
- fire-engineering reports, briefs, peer reviews and acceptance records;
- fire-safety schedules, annual statements, inspection and impairment records;
- architectural egress, accessibility and compartmentation information;
- structural fire-resistance and facade information;
- hydraulic fire-water and pump information;
- mechanical smoke-control and damper information;
- electrical detection, occupant-warning, emergency-power and control
  information;
- dangerous-goods, process, storage-height, rack and operational information;
- existing surveys, defects, penetration registers and test results;
- commissioning scripts, cause-and-effect matrices and handover records.

Record revision and approval status. A concept fire strategy, tender drawing
and approved construction document have different authority.

## Compliance strategy and responsibility

Establish a coordinated fire-safety basis that identifies:

- building classifications and mixed-use interfaces;
- applicable construction and compartment strategy;
- egress assumptions, occupant characteristics and accessibility interfaces;
- deemed-to-satisfy provisions and proposed performance solutions;
- fire-resistance and structural-fire inputs;
- active and passive measures;
- emergency-service access and facilities;
- maintenance, testing and operational assumptions;
- approval, peer-review and consultation gates.

Maintain a responsibility matrix showing who defines, designs, coordinates,
installs, inspects, tests, certifies and maintains each measure. No discipline
drawing should silently change the accepted strategy.

## Egress and occupant safety

Coordinate paths of travel, exits, stairs, doors, hardware, smoke separation,
signage, lighting, accessibility, security and operational management. Verify
occupancy and use assumptions against the brief. Storage, furniture, security
turnstiles or tenancy layouts must not be treated as outside the fire strategy
when they affect egress.

Where evacuation depends on staff procedures, staged evacuation, refuges,
assisted evacuation or operator intervention, define the responsible
operational adviser and the evidence required before occupation.

## Passive fire protection

Manage:

- fire-rated walls, floors, shafts, ceilings and structural elements;
- fire and smoke doors, shutters, dampers, access panels and hardware;
- service penetrations, control joints and perimeter interfaces;
- tested systems, permitted substrates and installation constraints;
- labels, traceability, inspection access and photographic records;
- coordination of late services changes and rectification.

Use a penetration and passive-fire register where scale or risk justifies it.
Do not approve an untested combination by analogy; refer departures to the
responsible designer and certifier.

## Active systems and water supply

Coordinate detection, alarm, occupant warning, sprinkler, hydrant, hose reel,
pump, tank, special suppression and monitoring systems that apply. Confirm
water-supply evidence, authority interfaces, equipment access, drainage,
electrical supply, monitoring and maintenance.

Industrial storage, process hazards, cold environments, batteries, high-rack
storage and dangerous goods require project-specific specialist advice. A
generic warehouse assumption is not a design basis.

## Smoke control, controls and cause and effect

The accepted fire strategy should drive the cause-and-effect matrix. Identify
every input, output, time delay, permissive, override, indication and fail
state across:

- detection and occupant warning;
- smoke exhaust, pressurisation, fans and dampers;
- lifts, doors, access control and security;
- mechanical shutdown or fire mode;
- power, generation and essential distribution;
- process shutdown and specialist systems.

Define authorship, design reviews, software responsibility, point-to-point
checks, witnessing and acceptance. Resolve conflicts before integrated testing.

## Existing buildings, refurbishment and remediation

Before altering an occupied building:

- verify installed measures against records and current surveys;
- identify undocumented penetrations, defects and inaccessible areas;
- determine approval implications of altered use, layout or services;
- sequence temporary measures and system impairments;
- maintain safe egress, detection, warning and emergency access;
- define isolations, notifications, fire watch and restoration tests;
- coordinate work with annual testing and building operations.

An existing fire-safety statement or certificate does not prove that all
physical conditions remain unchanged or effective.

## Consultant RFP minimum returnables

For fire engineering or fire-safety consultancy, request:

- scope understanding, pathway assumptions and exclusions;
- evidence and surveys required;
- fire-engineering brief, report and approval deliverables where applicable;
- consultation and peer-review scope;
- marked-up strategy plans, schedules and interface matrices;
- review of architectural, structural and services design;
- construction inspections, substitutions and RFI response;
- passive-fire and essential-measures documentation requirements;
- commissioning plan, cause-and-effect review and integrated-test witnessing;
- completion, occupation and fire-safety schedule support;
- team, competence, registrations, insurance, programme, fees and variation
  triggers.

State which party owns the final coordinated fire-safety strategy and how
comments are closed.

## Construction verification and integrated testing

Plan hold points for fire-rated construction, concealed penetrations, dampers,
doors, water supplies, controls interfaces and any project-specific critical
measure. Keep product, installer, inspection and test evidence linked to
locations.

Integrated testing should use approved scripts and defined normal, fire,
failure and recovery modes. Record expected outcome, actual result, witness,
defect and retest. A collection of individual trade certificates is not proof
that integrated behaviour has been tested.

## Handover and operations

Collect approved strategy documents, fire-safety schedules, as-builts,
penetration and asset registers, certificates, test results, cause-and-effect,
settings, impairment procedures, O&M manuals, training and outstanding
conditions. Confirm ownership of recurrent testing, defects and deferred
performance tests.

## PMP contribution

State the fire-safety pathway and evidence, responsible parties, performance
solutions, authority and peer-review gates, existing-building risks, design
interfaces, passive-fire controls, impairments, construction hold points,
integrated testing, occupation dependencies and unresolved assumptions.

## Common failures and low-confidence flags

Common failures are late classification decisions, performance solutions not
carried into trade documents, unowned passive-fire interfaces, security
conflicting with egress, cause and effect developed after installation,
uncontrolled system impairments, inaccessible dampers or valves, fragmented
testing and missing location-based records.

Flag low confidence when classification, approved fire strategy, fire-safety
schedule, existing-system survey, water-supply evidence, process or storage
profile, performance-solution status, cause and effect, approval pathway or
test-acceptance authority is missing.
