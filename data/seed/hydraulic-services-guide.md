# Hydraulic Services Guide

## Purpose and professional boundary

Use this guide to frame project controls and procurement questions for hydraulic
services. It is not a design, calculation, certification, authority approval or
substitute for the responsible hydraulic designer, certifier, utility,
water authority or licensed plumbing contractor.

The project lead should establish what systems are affected, who owns each
design and approval obligation, what existing information can be relied on, and
what must be investigated before scope and fee are fixed.

Applicable legislation, codes, authority requirements and current standards
must be verified for the project jurisdiction and approval pathway. Do not infer
a particular approval, regulated-design or authority process from the project
state alone.

## When to activate this guide

Activate the guide when project evidence or the proposed work involves:

- amenities, kitchens, kitchenettes, breakout areas, laundries or wet areas;
- potable, non-potable, recycled or rainwater services;
- sanitary plumbing, sewer, trade waste, grease arrestors or neutralisation;
- roof drainage, stormwater, overflow, OSD or charged systems;
- natural gas, LPG or specialist gases within hydraulic scope;
- hot-water generation, circulation, tempering or thermostatic mixing;
- pumps, tanks, meters, backflow prevention, pressure control or leak detection;
- landlord or base-building connections, risers, shutdowns or capacity;
- fire-water interfaces where responsibility must be separated from fire
  services design;
- refurbishment, retained services, live occupation or staged works;
- authority applications, inspections, certification or completion records.

Do not activate every system merely because the consultant is hydraulic. Scope
must follow the active project evidence. Systems not affected should be
identified as excluded or optional rather than included by habit.

## Source hierarchy and evidence discipline

Use sources in this order:

1. Current active-project evidence: brief, lease and fit-out guide, surveys,
   landlord information, drawings, schedules, authority records and current
   consultant advice.
2. Responsible-designer, certifier and authority requirements for the project.
3. Applicable legislation, NCC provisions and current standards verified for
   the jurisdiction.
4. This platform guide as general project-management and procurement guidance.

Label this guide as platform guidance, never project evidence. If project
documents conflict, disclose the conflict and seek confirmation from the party
responsible for the affected interface.

## Hydraulic evidence sweep

Before fixing scope, seek where relevant:

- project brief, room data sheets, occupancy and operating requirements;
- current architectural plans, reflected ceiling plans and fixture schedules;
- landlord fit-out guide, lease interface schedule and design-review protocol;
- existing hydraulic drawings, as-builts, surveys and services reports;
- water, sewer, stormwater and gas authority records or diagrams;
- base-building riser, branch, meter, isolation and connection information;
- available flow, pressure, capacity and condition information;
- existing hot-water plant, pumps, tanks, backflow devices and trade-waste
  equipment;
- structural, fire, civil, mechanical, electrical and waterproofing information;
- planning, certifier, authority and regulated-design requirements;
- hazardous-material, access, shutdown and occupied-building constraints;
- previous RFIs, defects, maintenance issues and incident records.

Record whether each source is current, verified, indicative or unavailable.
Existing drawings do not prove capacity, condition, location or compliance.

## System map

### Potable and non-potable water

Confirm affected fixtures, demand, available pressure and flow, connection
points, isolation, pressure control, backflow prevention, metering, water
quality, accessibility, maintenance and tenant/landlord responsibility.

For refurbishment work, identify whether retained branches can be reused and
what testing or investigation is required before reliance.

### Sanitary plumbing and sewer

Confirm fixture loads, grades, venting, connection levels, stack and branch
capacity, access for maintenance, pump requirements, acoustic treatment,
penetrations and authority interfaces.

Do not assume that a nearby stack has capacity or that an architectural layout
can be connected without survey.

### Hot water

Confirm load profile, generation type, storage or instantaneous duty,
circulation, temperature control, scald protection, energy source, plant space,
condensate or relief drainage, metering and maintenance access.

### Trade waste

Activate trade-waste scope only where the evidenced use requires it. Confirm
fixtures, contaminants, pre-treatment, sampling points, arrestors, authority
agreements, maintenance access and responsibility for ongoing servicing.

Ordinary office kitchenettes should not be described as commercial kitchens
without evidence.

### Stormwater and roof drainage

Activate stormwater design where the works affect roofs, terraces, catchments,
outlets, overflows, external works, below-ground drainage or detention systems.
Internal tenancy fit-outs should not automatically procure whole-building
stormwater design.

Where affected, confirm design rainfall basis, catchments, primary and overflow
paths, discharge points, OSD interfaces, flood or overland-flow constraints,
structural loads, waterproofing and safe failure paths.

### Gas

Activate gas scope only where gas use is evidenced. Confirm demand, connection,
metering, regulators, ventilation, isolation, flue or combustion-air
interfaces, hazardous areas, detection and authority requirements.

### Fire-water boundary

Hydraulic and fire-services responsibilities vary by appointment. The RFP must
state whether hydrants, hose reels, sprinklers, pumps, tanks and boosters are:

- excluded and designed by the fire-services consultant;
- included within the hydraulic consultant's appointment;
- split by system or design stage; or
- retained base-building systems requiring coordination only.

Never let a reference to sprinklers or fire water silently transfer fire design,
certification or testing responsibility to the hydraulic consultant.

## Refurbishment and commercial fit-out overlay

For tenancy refurbishment and commercial fit-out work, focus first on:

- amenities, kitchenettes, breakout points and any specialist wet areas;
- proposed occupancy and sanitary-fixture demand;
- existing branch, stack, riser and hot-water capacity;
- landlord connection, shutdown, permit and inspection requirements;
- tenant, landlord, base-building and head-contractor demarcations;
- slab and wall penetrations, core drilling, fire stopping and waterproofing;
- ceiling-space congestion, access panels and coordination with retained
  services;
- occupied-building staging, temporary services and out-of-hours work;
- metering and isolation needed for tenancy operation and future maintenance;
- make-good, as-built and landlord handover requirements.

Stormwater, trade waste, gas, OSD and whole-building infrastructure upgrades are
conditional. Include them only when the evidence or investigation demonstrates
an impact.

## Consultant request for fee proposal

An RFP for a hydraulic-services consultant should ask the consultant to:

1. Review current evidence and identify missing surveys, records, tests and
   design inputs before accepting the existing-services basis.
2. Confirm the systems affected, design criteria, demand and capacity
   assumptions, applicable requirements and proposed investigations.
3. Define scope and deliverables by project stage, including concept, design
   development, approval, tender, construction and handover as applicable.
4. Separate tenant, landlord, base-building, authority, fire-services,
   contractor-designed and client-direct responsibilities.
5. Coordinate architecture, structure, civil, fire, mechanical, electrical,
   waterproofing, acoustic and equipment interfaces.
6. State drawing, calculation, specification, schedule, model, certification,
   declaration, submission and authority responsibilities.
7. Include meetings, surveys, design reviews, landlord and authority responses,
   tender queries, submittal reviews, RFIs, inspections and defects allowances.
8. Define testing, commissioning, witnessing, training, as-built, O&M and
   completion-record review.
9. State exclusions, optional services, personnel, programme, fee stages,
   hourly rates, expenses and required client inputs.

Request a responsibility schedule whenever fire water, civil/stormwater,
contractor design, landlord works or authority submissions may be split.

## Consultant deliverables

Select only deliverables applicable to the appointment:

- existing-services due-diligence or investigation report;
- hydraulic design-basis report and responsibility schedule;
- fixture, demand, capacity, pressure, flow and drainage calculations;
- coordinated plans, schematics, details and penetration information;
- fixture, equipment, valve, meter and connection schedules;
- specifications and performance requirements;
- landlord, certifier and authority submission packages;
- design certificates, declarations and completion records where required;
- tender issue, construction issue and revision registers;
- tender clarifications and tender-return review;
- construction RFIs, technical submittal and shop-drawing reviews;
- inspection, test and defect records;
- commissioning, training, as-built and O&M review;
- final handover and outstanding-items schedule.

## Hydraulic trade tender

A plumbing or hydraulic trade tender procures construction work and must not be
copied from the consultant RFP. Establish whether it is construct-only,
design-and-construct, contractor-designed in part, or dependent on investigation
of existing services.

Address supply, installation, supports, sleeves, penetrations, fire stopping,
waterproofing, access, shutdowns, temporary services, permits, testing,
flushing, disinfection, pressure testing, commissioning, authority attendance,
certification, training, warranties, spares, as-builts and O&M manuals.

## Design review and coordination controls

Review hydraulic documentation for:

- stated design basis, inputs, assumptions and unresolved decisions;
- consistency between plans, schematics, schedules, calculations and
  specifications;
- verified connection locations, levels, capacities and access;
- coordination of fixtures, joinery, structure, ceilings and equipment;
- falls, vents, overflows, tundishes, relief discharges and safe termination;
- penetrations, sleeves, fire stopping, acoustic treatment and waterproofing;
- isolation, metering, maintainability and replacement access;
- tenant/landlord, fire/hydraulic, civil/hydraulic and consultant/contractor
  responsibility boundaries;
- approval, certification, inspection and completion requirements;
- testing and commissioning readiness.

## Construction, testing and handover

Before concealment, verify applicable hold points for set-out, penetrations,
in-wall and above-ceiling services, below-ground drainage, waterproofing
interfaces and fire stopping.

Close-out should address applicable pressure, leakage, flow, temperature,
backflow, drainage, pump, controls and integrated testing; authority and
landlord sign-off; defect completion; asset labels; training; warranties;
as-built drawings; O&M manuals; certificates and maintenance requirements.

## Common failure modes

- Scoping every hydraulic system instead of the systems affected by the project.
- Assuming existing drawings prove capacity, condition or exact location.
- Omitting investigation and then pricing design on unverified connections.
- Mixing fire-services and hydraulic responsibilities.
- Ignoring landlord permits, shutdowns, riser access and design review.
- Treating kitchenette plumbing as commercial trade waste without evidence.
- Including whole-building stormwater design in an internal fit-out by default.
- Missing sanitary-demand impacts from increased occupancy.
- Failing to coordinate penetrations, fire stopping, waterproofing and access.
- Requesting drawings without calculations, specifications or certification.
- Omitting tender, construction, testing and handover allowances from the fee.

## Low-confidence and escalation flags

Escalate and keep scope provisional where:

- no reliable existing-services information is available;
- capacity, pressure, flow, levels or connection points are unverified;
- authority or landlord responsibilities conflict;
- fire-water responsibility is unclear;
- specialist trade waste, medical, laboratory or process systems are proposed;
- flood, OSD, roof drainage or external civil interfaces are material;
- the project remains occupied during intrusive investigation or shutdowns;
- the approval, certification or regulated-design pathway is unresolved.
