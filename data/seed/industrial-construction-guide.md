# Industrial Construction Guide

## Purpose and professional boundary

Use this guide as the industrial-class overlay for project planning and
consultant procurement. Industrial is a diverse building class, not a synonym
for warehouse. A logistics shed, food plant, workshop, cold store and data
centre can have fundamentally different hazards, process loads, approvals,
commissioning regimes and operational consequences.

This guide is platform guidance, not project evidence. It does not select a
building classification, design a process, confirm a dangerous-goods threshold,
assess a hazardous area, certify compliance or warrant operational performance.
Those conclusions belong to the responsible designers, authorities, certifier,
contractors and operator. Verify the current NCC edition, NSW legislation,
planning controls, authority requirements, referenced standards and approval
conditions for the actual project.

## Activation and subtype control

Activate this guide when the project class is `industrial` and the work type is
`new`, `refurb` or `extend`. Before using subtype-specific guidance, identify
the supported project subtype from evidence:

- warehouse, distribution centre or last-mile logistics;
- manufacturing, assembly, workshop or maintenance facility;
- food, beverage, temperature-controlled or cold-chain facility;
- data centre or other mission-critical technical facility;
- laboratory, clean production or another controlled environment;
- resource recovery, energy, chemical or other process-intensive facility.

If the subtype is missing, mixed or novel, record it as an early decision. Do
not transfer cost, programme, fire, services or commissioning assumptions from
another subtype. Specialist regulated environments require a project-specific
reference and competent advice; label guidance low confidence until obtained.

## Evidence sweep

Before writing a PMP or consultant RFP, seek:

- the operational brief, process narrative, production or logistics model,
  operating hours, staffing, throughput, storage profile and expansion plan;
- surveys, geotechnical and contamination information, existing services,
  easements, flood and bushfire constraints, vehicle access and authority data;
- intended building classifications, ancillary uses and fire-compartment
  strategy;
- equipment lists, loads, utilities, heat rejection, drainage, trade waste,
  compressed air, gases, fuels, data and resilience requirements;
- dangerous-goods inventories, hazardous-material information and any
  hazardous-area, fire-engineering or emergency-planning advice;
- planning consent, environmental obligations, licences and approval
  conditions;
- landlord, operator, vendor, consultant and contractor responsibility
  matrices;
- shutdown windows, temporary-service needs and business-continuity controls
  for live sites;
- performance, validation, commissioning, training and handover requirements.

Record each source's author, revision, date and issue purpose. Treat equipment
vendor information as an input requiring coordinated design, not as proof that
the whole facility will perform.

## Operational brief and basis of design

Turn the operator's requirements into testable criteria. At minimum resolve:

- inbound, storage, production, outbound, waste and personnel flows;
- vehicle types, swept paths, queuing, docks, yard operations and pavement
  demands;
- clear heights, grid, floor flatness, loading, vibration and equipment zones;
- environmental conditions, cleanliness, temperature, humidity, pressure,
  acoustics and lighting where relevant;
- uptime, redundancy, maintainability, replacement routes and future capacity;
- security zones, access control, surveillance and network responsibility;
- amenities, offices and other ancillary occupancies;
- process interfaces and the boundary between base building, fitout, equipment
  vendor and operator systems.

Keep an assumptions register for values not confirmed by the operator. An
aspirational phrase such as "future-proof" is not a criterion until it has a
defined capacity, space allowance, interface or acceptance test.

## Site, planning and authority interfaces

Industrial sites commonly turn on interfaces outside the building footprint.
Coordinate:

- heavy-vehicle access, road upgrades, loading operations and emergency access;
- stormwater quantity and quality, flood response, erosion and sediment control;
- water, sewer, trade waste, electricity, gas and telecommunications capacity;
- noise, odour, light spill, emissions, operating hours and neighbour impacts;
- earthworks, retaining, contamination and acid-sulfate-soil controls where
  relevant;
- fire-water supply, hydrant access and emergency-service interfaces;
- planning, environmental, utility and work-health-and-safety approvals.

Do not state that an authority has capacity or accepted a solution unless
current written project evidence says so. Programme applications, design
reviews, agreements, augmentation and inspections as explicit dependencies.

## Building, process and services coordination

Use a design responsibility matrix to separate:

- architectural envelope and circulation;
- structural frame, slabs, equipment supports and dynamic loads;
- civil works, pavements, drainage and utilities;
- fire strategy, suppression, smoke control and emergency response;
- electrical supply, distribution, generation, UPS, controls and metering;
- mechanical comfort, ventilation, process exhaust, refrigeration and heat
  rejection;
- hydraulic water, sanitary drainage, process water and trade waste;
- process equipment, vendor packages and specialist controls;
- ICT, operational technology, security and systems integration.

Every vendor package should identify design inputs, loads, connection points,
spatial zones, access, builder's work, controls, test requirements and the party
responsible for end-to-end performance. Coordinate penetration, maintainability
and replacement access before construction issue.

## Procurement and packaging

Select packaging from the actual risk allocation. Potential packages include
site remediation and earthworks, civil and utility works, base building,
process equipment, refrigeration, automation, racking, specialist fire
protection and integrated commissioning.

For each early or vendor package, define:

- design status and the information the package may rely on;
- scope boundaries and interfaces with the head contract;
- programme dates, access and temporary works;
- authority, testing and certification obligations;
- warranties, spare parts, training and records;
- novation or coordination obligations, if any;
- treatment of operator changes and vendor data after award.

Avoid procuring long-lead equipment against an unapproved design basis or
building geometry. If early commitment is necessary, identify the retained
client risk and the change-control gate.

## Construction in operational environments

For refurbishment or extension of a live facility, the PMP should control:

- intrusive surveys and verified isolation points;
- possession areas, barriers and access segregation;
- shutdown requests, approvals, rehearsals and rollback plans;
- temporary power, fire protection, environmental control and production
  support;
- contamination, hygiene, dust, noise and foreign-matter controls;
- hot work, energisation, lifting and work near operating equipment;
- daily operational handbacks and evidence of safe restoration.

The operator should nominate an authorised interface for permits and shutdown
acceptance. Never infer that a routine construction methodology is compatible
with a live process.

## Commissioning and operational readiness

Plan commissioning from the brief, not at the end of construction. Develop:

- a system and asset breakdown;
- inspection and test plans, factory and site acceptance tests;
- pre-commissioning, energisation and safe-start procedures;
- controls point-to-point testing and cause-and-effect verification;
- integrated systems testing under normal, degraded and emergency modes;
- performance, load, reliability or validation testing appropriate to the
  subtype;
- defects, tuning, seasonal testing and deferred-test controls;
- operator training, drills, spares, warranties and maintenance mobilisation;
- asset data, O&M manuals, as-builts, settings, licences and test records.

Define who authors, witnesses, accepts and closes each test. Practical
completion should not silently substitute for operational acceptance.

## PMP contribution

An industrial PMP contribution should state:

- confirmed subtype, operating model and evidence;
- approval, utility and property dependencies;
- the design responsibility and interface matrix;
- live-site and operational-continuity controls;
- procurement packages and long-lead decisions;
- project-specific design and construction hold points;
- commissioning, validation and operational-readiness gates;
- unresolved assumptions, owner decisions and specialist appointments.

## Consultant RFP minimum returnables

Ask each consultant to return:

- scope understanding, exclusions and information relied upon;
- proposed investigations and missing inputs;
- discipline deliverables by project stage;
- design criteria and interfaces they will own or coordinate;
- meetings, workshops, reviews and authority engagement included;
- programme, dependencies and client decision dates;
- construction-phase inspections, submittal reviews and RFI allowance;
- commissioning, witnessing and handover services;
- team, experience, professional registrations and insurance;
- fee breakdown, reimbursables, assumptions and variation triggers.

Require subtype experience where the facility has process, hygiene, dangerous
goods, hazardous areas, high availability or specialist validation needs.

## Common failures and low-confidence flags

Common failures are an unverified operational brief, treating industrial as a
generic shed, late utility applications, incomplete vendor interfaces, slabs
designed before equipment data, unplanned shutdowns, fragmented controls,
unassigned integrated testing and occupation before operational documentation
is complete.

Flag low confidence when the subtype, process loads, dangerous-goods inventory,
utility capacity, fire strategy, existing-asset condition, live-site rules,
vendor data or acceptance criteria are absent. Do not fill those gaps with a
warehouse precedent or general model knowledge.
