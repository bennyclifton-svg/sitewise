# Institution Construction Guide

## Purpose and professional boundary

Use this guide as the institution-class overlay for project planning and
consultant procurement. Institution is a diverse building class, not a synonym
for school or hospital. Early childhood, school, tertiary, hospital, medical
centre, civic, religious and secure facilities have different occupants,
approvals, operating calendars, security regimes and governance.

This guide is platform guidance, not project evidence. It does not select a
building classification, certify education or health approval, confirm a
Working with Children Check or security clearance, assess infection-control
risk, warrant accessibility performance or invent authority acceptance. Those
conclusions belong to the responsible designers, authorities, certifier,
operators, clinicians and client. Verify the current NCC edition, NSW
legislation, planning controls, department standards, authority requirements,
referenced standards and approval conditions for the actual project. Default
to NSW unless evidence names another jurisdiction. Do not treat one subtype as
representative of all others.

Common pitfalls:
- Treating institution as a generic Class 9b assembly building.
- Copying hospital infection-control or school child-protection controls onto a civic or religious project.
- Stating that an authority has accepted a solution without current written project evidence.

## Activation and subtype control

Activate this guide when the project class is `institution` and the work type
is `new`, `refurb`, `extend` or `remediation`. Identify the subtype from
evidence before using subtype-specific guidance:

- `education_early_childhood` — education and care / childcare;
- `education_primary_secondary` — public or independent school;
- `education_tertiary` — university, TAFE or other campus teaching/research;
- `healthcare_hospital` — hospital, including emergency and theatres;
- `healthcare_medical_centre` — medical centre or GP/specialist tenancy;
- `healthcare_clinic_allied` — allied health clinic;
- `government_civic` — civic, library, community hub or council facility;
- `religious` — place of worship and associated halls;
- `other` — including corrections and other secure facilities where evidence
  supports that use.

If the subtype is missing, mixed or novel, record it as an early decision. Do
not transfer clinical, education, civic or secure-facility assumptions across
subtypes. Specialist regulated environments require a project-specific
reference and competent advice; label guidance low confidence until obtained.

Common pitfalls:
- Activating school term constraints on a hospital or civic project.
- Applying Australasian Health Facility Guidelines to an education or civic brief.
- Treating `other` as leftover commercial fitout when evidence indicates a secure facility.

## Evidence sweep

Before writing a PMP or consultant RFP, seek the operational brief, occupant
profile, operating calendar and live-environment rules; surveys, existing-
building condition, services and authority data; intended classifications and
fire strategy; named department, health, education or corrections standards;
child protection, Working with Children Check, security-clearance and site-
separation requirements; public procurement and probity rules; community,
councillor, clinical, faculty, congregation or operator governance;
accessibility beyond minimum; specialist services (medical gases, infection
control, laboratories, fume cupboards, worship acoustics, anti-ligature);
staging and continuity controls; and commissioning, accreditation and handover
requirements.

Record each source's author, revision, date and issue purpose. Treat operator
or clinician preference as a design input, not as proof of occupation
acceptance.

Common pitfalls:
- Writing the PMP without the operating calendar or live-site rules.
- Missing the named department or health guideline that governs the subtype.
- Treating vendor or fitout sketches as evidence of authority approval.

## Public procurement and probity

Institutional work, especially civic, education and health, is often public
procurement. Identify the applicable NSW procurement framework, agency policy
and any ICAC or audit-office probity expectations from project evidence. Do
not invent a tender method or evaluation model.

Control advertised openness, documented evaluation criteria before market
approach, conflict-of-interest declarations, panel composition, separation of
advice and approval roles, treatment of incumbents and late information, and
records sufficient for audit, GIPA and councillor scrutiny. Probity is a
process control, not a label. If a probity adviser is appointed, define scope,
hold points and reporting line. Do not state that a process is
"probity-compliant" unless the appointed adviser or agency has recorded that
conclusion.

For religious or independent-school clients, still identify whether public
grants or mixed funding trigger public procurement duties. Staged construction
tied to fundraising is a cash-flow control, not a reason to skip competitive
process where the client is bound to one.

Common pitfalls:
- Running a public tender on unpublished evaluation criteria.
- Allowing incumbents or design consultants to evaluate packages they will bid.
- Treating a councillor briefing as a substitute for a documented procurement decision.

## Operating calendar and live-environment staging

The operating calendar often governs programme more than construction logic.
Resolve term dates, holiday shutdowns, examination periods, academic-calendar
handover, clinical hours, emergency-department continuity, worship times and
secure-facility routines from evidence. Do not assume a commercial shutdown
window.

Stage school works around the term and holiday programme, with staged
occupation so teaching can continue. Treat tertiary sites as a live campus
with student occupancy and research/lab continuity. For a live emergency
department, live ED staging is the governing problem: the department cannot
close, so each possession needs a clinical owner, infection-control review and
rollback. Programme civic work around public hours, religious work around
worship, and corrections around operational security. Never infer that a
routine construction methodology is compatible with a live institution. The
operator should nominate an authorised interface for permits, possessions and
daily handback.

Common pitfalls:
- Programming a school upgrade as a continuous commercial build through term.
- Assuming an emergency department can decant without a verified live-staging plan.
- Ignoring student occupancy, research shutdowns or worship times when setting possessions.

## Child protection, security clearance and site separation

Where children, patients or persons in custody occupy the site, construction
is a safeguarding and security problem as well as a WHS problem.

For early childhood and schools, apply child protection and site separation:
physical barriers, independent contractor access, induction, supervision
sightlines, and Working with Children Check or equivalent clearance for anyone
who may have contact with children. Do not rely on a contractor's generic
induction as proof of child-protection compliance.

For corrections and other secure facilities, require security clearance for
workers and materials, escorted movement, tool and vehicle control, and
operational security during works. Anti-ligature and secure detailing are
design obligations, not finishes extras. Keep workers, deliveries and waste
out of occupied education, clinical and secure zones unless a written
possession says otherwise.

Common pitfalls:
- Shared gates or amenities between contractors and children or patients.
- Commencing on a secure site before worker and material clearances are in place.
- Copying school fencing rules onto a hospital or correctional project without checking the named standard.

## Stakeholder governance

Identify the decision owner, the consultation set and the escalation path.
Typical sets include the department asset owner and school or centre director;
faculties, facilities and students on a live campus; clinical stakeholder
governance for hospitals (ED, theatres, infection control, engineering);
practitioners and landlords for medical-centre fitout; allied-health
operators; councillors, executives and the public for civic work;
congregation, trustees and volunteer labour for religious work; and
corrections operations plus the named authority for secure facilities.

Community consultation is not the same as councillor or public governance.
Record which forums can change scope and which can only comment. Do not treat
a workshop note as client instruction until the nominated owner confirms it.
Clinical stakeholder governance must include who signs staging, infection
control during construction, medical-gas interruptions and commissioning with
clinical staff.

Common pitfalls:
- Letting a user group rewrite the brief after tender without a change-control gate.
- Briefing councillors or clinicians without a single client instruction path.
- Omitting infection-control or operations from hospital staging decisions.

## Accessibility and inclusion beyond minimum

Plan accessibility beyond minimum. Deemed-to-satisfy NCC access provisions
and the Premises Standards are a floor, not the civic or education brief.
Many institution clients require inclusive wayfinding, hearing augmentation,
changing-places facilities, sensory-aware rooms, equitable staff amenities,
consult-room circulation and outdoor play access that exceed the DTS set.

For medical centres and allied health, consult-room compliance and
accessibility, and acoustic privacy between consult and treatment rooms, are
occupancy requirements. Civic buildings should state long-life durability and
inclusion as explicit criteria. Do not certify that a design meets the
Disability Discrimination Act, Premises Standards or a department inclusion
policy. Record the named standard, the access consultant's scope, and which
items are client enhancements beyond the certifier's minimum.

Common pitfalls:
- Equating a DTS access check with a client brief for accessibility beyond minimum.
- Ignoring consult-room accessibility and acoustic privacy in medical and allied-health fitout.
- Leaving civic inclusion to FF&E after the structure and sanitary layout are fixed.

## Specialist facility standards

Identify the named standard from evidence. Do not import another subtype's
guideline.

Early childhood: the Education and Care Services National Regulations govern
space per child (indoor and outdoor), fencing, supervision sightlines and
approval before operation. Ligature and safety detailing, finger-trap hardware
and outdoor play fencing are design controls.

Schools: apply the named Department of Education or equivalent standards,
existing-building interface and staged occupation. Child protection and site
separation remain in force for the whole programme.

Tertiary: coordinate live campus logistics, student occupancy, wet-lab
services, fume cupboards and research/lab interfaces.

Hospitals: use the Australasian Health Facility Guidelines where the health
agency names them. Control infection control during construction, medical
gases, clinical stakeholder governance, commissioning with clinical staff, and
live ED staging on an operational emergency department.

Medical centre and allied health: consult-room compliance, accessibility,
acoustic privacy, infection-control finishes and accreditation pathways named
in the brief. Medical gas only where evidence requires it.

Civic: public procurement and probity, community consultation, councillor and
public governance, accessibility and inclusion beyond minimum, and long-life
durability.

Religious: assembly occupancy egress, acoustic design for worship, and staged
construction tied to funding. Volunteer labour is a WHS and quality risk.

Corrections and other secure facilities: security clearance for workers and
materials, anti-ligature, operational security, escorted movement and the
named authority standards. Do not publish security detailing beyond what the
operator permits.

Common pitfalls:
- Using a hospital AusHFG room data sheet as a school or civic briefing tool.
- Omitting Education and Care Services National Regulations space-per-child and fencing checks.
- Treating religious assembly as a hall fitout without egress and worship-acoustic design.

## Construction, commissioning and handover

For live institutions, construction methodology is a clinical, educational or
security method as well as a building method. Control possession areas and
independent contractor access; infection control during construction,
including dust, aspergillus and water-system risks on health sites; noise,
vibration and hot-work limits against occupied teaching, worship, clinical or
custody areas; temporary fire, egress, medical gases and security; daily
handbacks; and staged occupation with the named operator's acceptance.

Plan commissioning from the brief. Hospitals require commissioning with
clinical staff, medical-gas verification and infection-control clearance
before occupation. Medical centres and clinics need finishes, acoustic and
accreditation evidence. Education facilities need staged occupation and defect
windows that respect terms. Civic handover should include inclusion features
and durability records. Religious staging may follow funding tranches; each
occupied stage still needs egress complete. Secure facilities require
operator-witnessed security, anti-ligature and locking tests before any
reduction in escort.

Practical completion should not silently substitute for operational,
clinical, education-authority or security acceptance.

Common pitfalls:
- Occupying a live ED, school block or custody wing on a builder's completion certificate alone.
- Commissioning medical gases or labs without the named clinical or research owner.
- Handing over before accessibility, inclusion and operating-calendar defects are closed.
