# Non-Residential Sustainability and Energy Guide

## Purpose and professional boundary

Use this guide for commercial and industrial energy and sustainability
workstreams. It complements, and does not replace, the residential BASIX and
NatHERS guide.

This guide does not assess NCC compliance, predict an energy rating, certify a
Green Star or NABERS outcome, perform regulated modelling, calculate carbon
claims or warrant operational performance. Use current project certificates,
approved models, rating-tool rules, evidence requirements and advice from the
responsible energy modeller, sustainability consultant, certifier and rating
assessor.

Schemes and regulations change. Verify the edition, pathway, scope, benchmark
period, rating boundary and evidence rules that apply to the project.

## Activation

Activate relevant sections when the project includes:

- non-residential NCC energy-efficiency compliance;
- an energy-use, greenhouse, water or indoor-environment target;
- NABERS, Green Star or another client-nominated rating;
- all-electric design, fossil-fuel removal or demand management;
- renewable generation, energy storage or EV infrastructure;
- submetering, monitoring, tuning or performance reporting;
- embodied-carbon, responsible-product or construction-waste commitments;
- refurbishment of envelope, lighting, HVAC, hot water or controls.

Do not add a voluntary rating target that the brief and client have not adopted.

## Evidence sweep and target register

Seek:

- confirmed building classification, uses, areas, hours and occupancy;
- client sustainability brief and investment or leasing commitments;
- planning conditions and current NCC compliance pathway;
- rating registrations, scorecards, technical manuals and assessor advice;
- energy models, input schedules, assumptions and design-review records;
- envelope, facade, lighting, mechanical, hydraulic and electrical data;
- existing utility bills, interval data, meter hierarchy and operational
  history for refurbishments;
- equipment schedules, controls sequences and commissioning plans;
- product declarations, quantity data and procurement requirements where
  embodied impacts are assessed;
- landlord, tenant and operator responsibility boundaries.

Create a target register showing the target, metric, boundary, baseline,
evidence, responsible party, design response, verification gate and status.
Keep mandatory compliance and voluntary aspirations distinct.

## Performance brief and design basis

Translate goals into measurable requirements. Resolve:

- regulated versus unregulated loads;
- tenancy, base-building, process and shared-service boundaries;
- climate, schedules, occupancy and internal-load assumptions;
- modelled design outcome and any operational target;
- peak demand, electrification and resilience constraints;
- comfort, ventilation and indoor-environment requirements;
- metering, data access and post-occupancy verification;
- change-control rules for assumptions and substitutions.

For industrial facilities, separate process energy from building energy and
identify who owns each. Do not compare assets or options whose boundaries and
operating profiles differ.

## NCC energy-efficiency coordination

The responsible designers and certifier should establish the applicable
compliance pathway. Coordinate:

- envelope thermal performance, glazing, shading, sealing and insulation;
- lighting power and controls;
- mechanical efficiency, controls and outside-air interfaces;
- hot-water and pumping systems;
- energy monitoring and metering;
- commissioning and evidence required by the selected pathway.

Maintain an input and evidence schedule so architectural and services changes
are assessed before issue. A model result is valid only for its recorded inputs
and methodology.

## Voluntary ratings and commitments

For NABERS, Green Star or another voluntary scheme:

- confirm registration, tool, version, rating scope and target;
- appoint the responsible accredited practitioner or assessor as required;
- map credits or rating inputs to deliverables and owners;
- identify design-stage evidence, construction evidence and operational data;
- confirm tenancy, base-building and whole-building boundaries;
- programme submission, review, challenge and certification periods;
- define the consequence and decision route if a target is at risk.

Do not use a design estimate as though it were a certified operational rating.
Make clear when an outcome depends on tenant loads, operator behaviour or a
future performance period.

## Electrification, generation and demand

Assess electrification as a coordinated building and network strategy. Address:

- heating, hot water, cooking and process loads proposed for conversion;
- supply capacity, maximum demand and augmentation;
- plant space, heat rejection, acoustic and maintenance implications;
- solar, storage, EV charging and load-management interfaces;
- backup power and resilience requirements;
- tariffs, metering, controls and operational capability;
- staged conversion and temporary systems in existing buildings.

No technology should be selected from an energy target alone. Confirm
functional performance, utility capacity and whole-system impacts with the
responsible designers.

## Metering, controls and operational performance

Develop a meter hierarchy aligned with rating and management boundaries.
Define:

- meters, points and data resolution;
- landlord, tenant, process and major-system separation;
- communications, naming, validation and data ownership;
- dashboards, alarms, trend logs and reporting responsibilities;
- controls sequences, set points, schedules and overrides;
- tuning, seasonal review and post-occupancy evaluation.

Meters without commissioned data pathways do not provide performance
verification. Controls intent must be expressed as testable sequences.

## Materials, embodied impacts and site practices

Where the brief includes embodied carbon, responsible products, circularity or
waste:

- define the assessment boundary and methodology;
- establish the quantity and product evidence required;
- distinguish product-specific evidence from generic factors;
- set substitution and approval controls;
- prevent double counting across structure, envelope, fitout and services;
- plan construction records, waste data and final reporting.

Avoid unsupported environmental claims. Refer product claims, life-cycle
assessment and certification to competent advisers and current programme rules.

## Consultant RFP minimum returnables

Request:

- target and compliance-pathway understanding;
- proposed methodology, tools, versions and evidence requirements;
- project registration or assessor services where relevant;
- modelling, reports, target register and input schedules by stage;
- design workshops and discipline review;
- planning, certifier and rating-body interfaces;
- procurement clauses, substitution review and construction evidence;
- commissioning, tuning and operational-data services;
- certification or post-occupancy scope and dependencies;
- team credentials, programme, fee breakdown, exclusions and variation
  triggers.

Name who integrates sustainability obligations across disciplines and who owns
the final submission.

## Construction, commissioning and handover

Track approved envelope details, insulation, sealing, glazing, equipment
efficiencies, controls, metering, product evidence and substitutions. Use
inspection and test plans for items that will become concealed.

Commission meters, trend logs, controls and plant sequences under representative
modes. Handover models, certificates, evidence registers, settings, manuals,
training, target status and a tuning plan. Assign responsibility for data review
and corrections after occupation.

## PMP contribution

State confirmed mandatory and voluntary targets, boundaries, compliance
pathway, modeller and assessor responsibilities, input and evidence controls,
design-review gates, utility dependencies, procurement requirements,
commissioning, operational verification and unresolved assumptions.

## Common failures and low-confidence flags

Common failures are mixing residential and non-residential schemes, targets
without a defined boundary, design changes not returned to the model, landlord
and tenant loads conflated, voluntary targets treated as assured, uncommissioned
meters, controls without tuning ownership and product claims without compliant
evidence.

Flag low confidence when the building use, target, scheme version, rating
boundary, model inputs, existing energy data, tenant or process loads, utility
capacity, evidence owner or post-occupancy responsibility is missing.
