# NSW Building Remediation Cost Breakdown Reference

This reference supplies a practice-level cost structure for NSW residential,
commercial and industrial building remediation or rectification. It covers the
commercial shape of investigations, access, temporary protection, defined work
packages, making good, verification and close-out.

It does not diagnose a defect, attribute liability, establish a statutory
obligation, select a remedy or prove causation. Those positions must come from
competent project-specific investigation, design, legal advice and authority
evidence. Keep confirmed scope, provisional scope and excluded scope visibly
separate.

This is structure only. It is not a rate pack, benchmark, project budget,
remedial design, tender recommendation, active-project evidence or
source-of-truth cost plan. Amounts, quantities, rates, percentages, access
assumptions and programme effects must be evidenced or marked TBC.

## Workbook-Ready Groups

Use these top-level groups:

- investigations, design and statutory costs;
- access, enabling works and temporary protection;
- remediation construction works by defect or location package;
- occupant, operator, owner and authority costs;
- contingency and explicit provisional allowances.

Do not combine investigation, access and permanent rectification into one line
when they have different decision gates or procurement routes. Do not include
legal damages, insurance recoveries or liability allocations in a construction
subtotal.

## Remediation Cost Families

| Family | Typical cost item labels and boundaries |
| --- | --- |
| Investigations and evidence capture | Record review, surveys, opening-up, sampling, testing, monitoring, laboratory work, intrusive inspection, reinstatement of test locations and baseline condition records |
| Design, approvals and certification | Remedial design, specialist engineering, performance-solution inputs, certifier and authority interfaces, access approvals, permits and verification planning |
| Preliminaries and remediation controls | Site establishment, supervision, insurances, stakeholder liaison, exclusion zones, inductions, security, dust/noise/water controls, temporary services and reporting |
| Access and temporary works | Scaffolds, mast climbers, swing stages, elevated work platforms, gantries, crash decks, shoring, propping, weather protection, temporary egress and access certification |
| Isolation, decanting and protection | Service isolations, relocations, occupant decant, furniture or stock movement, protection, temporary facilities, business-continuity measures and reinstatement |
| Hazardous materials and controlled removal | Project-evidenced asbestos, lead, mould or other hazardous-material controls, removal, transport, disposal, clearance and records; do not infer contamination |
| Façade and cladding rectification | Removal, replacement, subframing, cavity barriers, membranes, flashings, sealants, fire stopping, windows, interfaces, sample panels and façade testing |
| Waterproofing and water-ingress rectification | Investigation openings, substrate repair, membranes, joints, flashings, drainage, wet areas, roofs, podiums, balconies, planters, flood or hose testing and making good |
| Concrete and structural rectification | Breakout, corrosion treatment, reinforcement replacement, patch repair, crack injection, strengthening, bearing or connection work, protective coatings, propping and verification |
| Fire and life-safety rectification | Compartmentation, penetrations, fire doors, dampers, detection, warning systems, sprinklers, hydrants, smoke-control interfaces, signage, testing and close-out evidence |
| Building-services rectification | Mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, controls, communications or security replacement and alteration; isolations, temporary services, testing, balancing and commissioning |
| Internal finishes and making good | Linings, ceilings, joinery, tiling, flooring, painting, fixtures, cleaning and reinstatement affected by access or permanent works |
| External works and drainage rectification | Stormwater, paving, retaining, subsoil drainage, landscaping, external structures and affected public or common areas |
| Testing, monitoring and verification | Hold-point inspections, samples, independent testing, water testing, fire and services testing, monitoring, commissioning and compliance records |
| Handover and post-completion | As-builts, product data, warranties, maintenance plans, training, defects close-out, post-completion inspections and project-evidenced monitoring periods |
| Unresolved or alternative remedy package | Separately priced options for unverified extent, alternative access, trial area, staged opening-up or alternative remedial design; never blend options into the base total |

## Package and Location Structure

Below the common families, split costs by the project's traceable unit:

- building, stage, elevation, level, lot, tenancy, room or defect type;
- confirmed quantity versus provisional extent;
- intrusive investigation versus permanent works;
- occupied-hours versus after-hours or shutdown works;
- base scope versus separately approved option.

Each row should identify the evidence or assumption that defines its extent.
Preserve the diagnostic consultant's location and defect identifiers when they
are suitable stable lookup keys.

## Consultant and Tender Pricing Returnables

Requests should separately price:

- desktop review, non-intrusive inspection and intrusive investigation;
- sampling, laboratory testing and opening-up assumptions;
- remedial options, design stages and authority/certifier support;
- access design and temporary works;
- tender support and construction-phase inspections;
- witness points, testing, certification and close-out deliverables;
- return visits, monitoring and post-completion review;
- disbursements, exclusions, options and approved-variation hourly rates.

Contractor returnables should expose quantities, access assumptions, temporary
works, exclusions, making-good extent, disposal, testing and option pricing
where the procurement strategy permits. Never request invented precision where
the investigation has not established the extent.

## Cost-Control Prompts

PMPs and cost plans should identify:

- the approved cost limit and whether legal, insurance, decant and business
  costs are inside or outside it;
- the current diagnostic confidence and outstanding investigations;
- hold points between opening-up, design confirmation and production work;
- access, occupation, shutdown and weather assumptions;
- the basis of provisional quantities and how they will be remeasured;
- responsibility for existing-condition damage and making good;
- contingency, latent-condition and scope-growth treatment;
- verification evidence required before completion.

All totals, option comparisons and contingencies must be calculated
deterministically by the workflow.

## Use Rules

- Use these families as a starting taxonomy, not as a diagnosis or scope.
- Preserve project-specific defect, location, tender and contract identifiers.
- Keep base scope, provisional scope and options separately totalled.
- Keep GST basis and occupant/client-direct costs explicit.
- Prefer TBC or a documented provisional allowance to unsupported quantities
  and rates.
- Describe platform knowledge as guidance, never as proof of the active defect.

## Boundary

This reference intentionally excludes:

- defect diagnosis, causation, liability, insurance and legal conclusions;
- contaminated-land remediation except where it is an evidenced interface to
  building works;
- market rates, benchmark $/m² figures, trade percentages or supplier pricing;
- a mandated access method, remedy, testing regime or approval pathway;
- any instruction to use another project as evidence.
