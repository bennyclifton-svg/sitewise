# NSW Commercial Base-Building Cost Breakdown Reference

This reference supplies a practice-level cost structure for new and extended
NSW office, shopping-centre and standalone-retail base buildings. It addresses
the landlord or developer building, common areas, site works and base-building
services. It does not define a tenant fit-out.

The project team must confirm the NCC classifications, development mix, tenancy
strategy, landlord design criteria, shell-and-core handover condition, public
domain obligations and authority requirements from active-project evidence.
The taxonomy subclass does not establish those facts.

This is structure only. It is not a rate pack, benchmark, project budget,
tender recommendation, active-project evidence or source-of-truth cost plan.
Amounts, quantities, rates, percentages and programme effects must be evidenced
or marked TBC.

## Workbook-Ready Groups

Use these top-level groups:

- fees, levies and statutory charges;
- consultants and investigations;
- base-building construction works;
- tenant, anchor, authority and client-direct works;
- contingency and explicit allowances.

Keep landlord base building, common areas, tenant works and client-direct
packages distinct. A lease contribution, incentive or tenant allowance is not
base-building construction merely because it affects the development budget.

## Base-Building Cost Families

| Family | Typical cost item labels and boundaries |
| --- | --- |
| Preliminaries and project controls | Site establishment, supervision, insurances, cranes and hoists, temporary services, logistics, traffic and public protection, staging and project-specific operational controls |
| Investigations, demolition and enabling works | Surveys, investigations, hazardous-materials provisions, demolition, diversions, protection, early works and temporary works |
| Siteworks, earthworks and retention | Clearing, excavation, spoil handling, shoring, dewatering, ground improvement, retaining structures and service corridors |
| Substructure and basements | Piling, footings, slabs, basement walls, tanking, ramps, loading areas and basement builder's work |
| Superstructure | Concrete, reinforcement, post-tensioning, precast, structural steel, timber where specified, stairs and structural temporary works |
| Façade and external envelope | Curtain wall, windows, shopfront shell, cladding, masonry, doors, louvres, canopies, balustrades, façade access and fire-stopping interfaces |
| Roofing and waterproofing | Roofing, membranes, drainage interfaces, plant-deck systems, podiums, terraces, planters, wet areas and testing |
| Core and common-area fit-out | Lobbies, corridors, stairs, common amenities, end-of-trip areas, management and back-of-house rooms, ceilings, finishes, doors and fixed joinery |
| Landlord retail and shell works | Mall areas, arcades, amenities, tenancy boundaries, shopfront-control interfaces, landlord provisions and shell handover works; tenant fit-out remains separate |
| Vertical transport | Passenger, goods, service and accessible lifts, escalators where applicable, finishes, controls, builder's work, testing and authority interfaces |
| Mechanical services | Central plant, air and water distribution, ventilation, smoke-control interfaces, tenant provisions, controls, metering, testing and commissioning |
| Electrical services | Incoming supply, substations, main distribution, standby systems where specified, common and external lighting, tenant provisions, metering, earthing and testing |
| Hydraulic services | Water, drainage, stormwater, gas where applicable, trade-waste provisions, hot water, pumps, tanks, tenancy provisions, metering and authority connections |
| Fire and life-safety services | Hydrants, hose reels, sprinklers, detection, warning and intercommunication systems, smoke-control interfaces, fire stopping, testing and certification |
| ICT, security and base-building technology | Carrier pathways, communications rooms, backbone cabling, access control, CCTV, intercom, building-management integration and operational-technology interfaces |
| External works and public domain | Roads, parking, loading, pavements, drainage, landscaping, lighting, fencing, signage, utility works, public-domain works and authority handbacks |
| Testing, commissioning and handover | Integrated testing, seasonal or deferred testing where evidenced, training, as-builts, O&M manuals, asset registers, warranties and defects close-out |
| Specialist commercial package (gap) | Anchor works, food-service infrastructure, cinema, major signage, high-security, specialist retail plant or other project-specific systems—separate and define from evidence |

## Shell, Tenancy and Authority Interfaces

For each tenancy or anchor type, state the documented handover condition and
identify which party carries:

- floor, wall, ceiling and shopfront finishes;
- incoming electrical capacity and distribution;
- mechanical capacity, outside air and controls;
- hydraulic, gas and trade-waste provisions;
- fire-service coverage and alteration;
- metering, communications and security pathways;
- loading, waste, grease-arrestor and back-of-house requirements;
- commissioning, certification and make-good obligations.

Show utility augmentation, public-domain works, authority assets, major tenant
works and client-direct technology as separate packages unless the approved
contract scope places them in the head contract.

## Consultant and Tender Pricing Returnables

Requests should separate:

- investigations and enabling works;
- concept, design development, approval and construction documentation;
- base-building, common-area and tenancy-interface deliverables;
- authority, utility, certifier and landlord/tenant coordination;
- façade, structure, fire, vertical transport and services coordination;
- tender and construction services with duration, meetings, inspections and
  reporting assumptions;
- commissioning, handover and defects services;
- disbursements, exclusions, options and approved-variation hourly rates.

Require respondents to declare which tenancy briefs, anchor criteria, existing
records and authority advice their price assumes.

## Cost-Control Prompts

PMPs and cost plans should identify:

- the approved cost limit and GST basis;
- the shell-and-core and common-area specification baseline;
- treatment of tenant allowances, incentives and client-direct packages;
- basement, façade, vertical-transport, utility and authority decisions;
- tenancy coordination and design-freeze gates;
- escalation, provisional sums, contingency and unresolved-interface treatment;
- review points at concept, design development, pre-tender, tender
  recommendation and post-contract forecast.

All arithmetic must be performed deterministically by the workflow.

## Use Rules

- Treat the families as a starting taxonomy, not a mandatory schedule.
- Preserve better project-specific QS, tender and contract labels.
- Keep exact approved item text stable for later workbook reconciliation.
- Keep the GST basis and tenant/base-building boundaries explicit.
- Prefer lump-sum TBC lines to unsupported quantities and rates.
- Record unresolved scope as an interface rather than assigning it silently.

## Boundary

This reference intentionally excludes:

- commercial tenancy fit-out, which has its own governed reference;
- hotels, food-and-beverage premises and other specialist operating models;
- market rates, benchmark $/m² figures, trade percentages or supplier pricing;
- any assumed tenant brief, lease position, authority scope or approval outcome;
- any instruction to use another project as evidence.
