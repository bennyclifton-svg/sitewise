## Four things that are easy to get wrong

1. **`A-000` is a drawing register inside the document register.** Correct practice, and a
   real double-counting hazard. It should appear as one sheet, not expand.

2. **Revisions are not all the same.** 18 sheets at Rev A, 14 at Rev B, 15 at Rev C. Rev C
   exists because the heritage setback was revised to 1.8 m before lodgement. A register
   that flattens everything to "current" loses the reason the set moved.

3. **Two disciplines, one firm.** Catchment Civil & Hydraulic authored both the `C-` and
   `H-` sets under different job numbers (`CCH-2604` and `CCH-2604-H`). Grouping by firm
   instead of discipline merges two separate packages.

4. **Issue purpose splits the set in two.** 33 sheets are *For Development Application*,
   14 are *For Construction*, issued in November 2025 with the CC. That distinction is what
   makes the register useful — it is the difference between what Council assessed and what
   the builder is building from.

## The gap worth surfacing

Five firms have documents on file and **no engagement evidence anywhere in the project** —
Larkin & Vale (survey), Stratum (geotechnical), Canopy (arborist), Solaris (BASIX and ESD)
and Redwood (QS cost summary). There are no fee proposals and no invoices for any of them.

That is deliberate, and it is what a real project looks like six months in. The correct
behaviour is to list them as document authors while keeping their appointment status
unevidenced. A report arriving does not prove anyone was engaged.

If those five appear as appointed consultants, that is the defect this step exists to catch.
