## Brief
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_03036dc31ad39b455c57e12e7f727438 -->
The physical brief is refurbishment works for solar and battery infrastructure at an existing live site. Capacity, panel or turbine count, battery storage, substation scope, power-source arrangement, site condition and hazardous-material condition are **Assumption** pending the owner brief, surveys and technical reports. The $4m budget is a planning figure only; its inclusions are not evidenced.
The brief must define the plant and mounting scope, existing-roof or ground-interface works, electrical connection and protection works, access routes, temporary works, shutdown windows, operational controls, testing, commissioning, handover data and restoration. Acceptance criteria are safe integration with continuing operations, approved design and authority requirements, tested system performance, complete commissioning records and owner acceptance.

## Planning and Compliance
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_b75822566cb5ec2a943d1e5fd9480cbb -->
Approval status, consent history, certifier appointment, authority interfaces and network requirements are not evidenced. Establish whether the works are exempt, require a CDC, DA or another statutory pathway before committing design or procurement dates. The selected pathway below is a working placeholder, not a statutory conclusion.
The NCC compliance strategy must be documented as Deemed-to-Satisfy (DtS), Performance Solution, or a defined combination. The design documentation should identify applicable building elements, electrical and fire/life-safety interfaces, access, egress, structural support, weatherproofing and energy requirements for the verified scope. Where a Performance Solution is proposed, identify the relevant assessment method, stakeholders and certifier acceptance process before design freeze.
State whether existing essential safety measures, fire-safety schedules, evacuation arrangements and building approvals are affected by plant installation, penetrations, battery enclosure or altered access. Fire-services scope, if triggered, requires specialist advice; no project-specific fire design is assumed. Obtain site, planning, environmental, heritage, bushfire, flood, contamination and network records before determining approval conditions or authority hold points.

## Programme

## Cost Planning
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_b75a8aa29efed747d6bd0893daf449a8 -->
The stated budget is $4m. Its currency, GST treatment, inclusions, exclusions, consultant costs, approvals, connection charges, escalation, contingency and client-supplied items are **Assumption**. Do not use it as a tender-control budget until a scope-based cost plan and risk allowance are agreed.
Prepare a cost plan aligned to the verified plant scope, structural and electrical interfaces, access and temporary works, safety controls, commissioning, documentation, approvals and restoration. Separately identify provisional or risk allowances for survey findings, contaminated or hazardous materials, network augmentation, structural strengthening, restricted access, shutdown work, fire-safety changes and programme escalation. Test long-lead equipment and connection assumptions before budget lock.
The project lead should maintain a committed-cost, forecast-final-cost, variation and contingency register. Each variation requires a defined scope, programme effect, cost basis and owner decision before instruction. Recommendation: reserve contingency until surveys, authority pathway and connection boundary are verified; do not absorb these uncertainties into an unsupported lump-sum comparison.

The live environment is the controlling delivery risk. What this means for the owner: access and outage constraints may determine feasible design, sequencing, cost and completion more than the nominal equipment installation. The project lead should require a single operational-interface plan covering authorised access, inductions, isolations, permits, work zones, emergency response, communications, contamination response and daily reinstatement. It should identify who can approve a shutdown, the notice period, fallback operations and the escalation route where an outage cannot proceed.
Risk owners must reassess the register at brief lock, approval submission, design freeze, tender recommendation, mobilisation and commissioning. A change to capacity, battery location, network arrangement, site condition or authority requirement is a trigger for scope, cost and programme review rather than an informal site instruction. Maintain detailed risk analysis and treatment evidence in the companion register.
