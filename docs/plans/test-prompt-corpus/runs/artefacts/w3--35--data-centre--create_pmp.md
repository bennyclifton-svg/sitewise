## Brief
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_829b41a3085056469c45fe8eb15e542f -->
The physical brief is a new Class 7b data centre planned for 15 MW IT load at redundancy tier 3. Included work packages are site clearance, bulk earthworks, substructure/foundations, superstructure, mechanical/HVAC, electrical/power, fire services, BMS/controls, security systems and ICT/structured cabling. The brief must establish the site boundary, utility points of connection, white-space and support-space areas, resilience topology, equipment vendor interfaces, security zoning, external works and operational handover requirements.
Acceptance criteria must include coordinated design documentation, authority and certifier acceptance, tested life-safety systems, integrated controls and security testing, commissioning evidence, operating manuals, training and defects close-out. PUE target, GFA, white-space area, power source, water strategy and performance criteria are **Assumption** pending owner and technical brief confirmation.

## Planning and Compliance
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_45741867da4a1cda68015185b594e255 -->
Approval pathway, consent status, land-use controls, certifier engagement and statutory instruments are **Not evidenced**. The first compliance gate is a site-specific planning and constraints review: address and title information, zoning and permissible use, development standards, easements, utility corridors, flood and bushfire mapping, contamination, heritage, biodiversity, access and servicing capacity. NSW-specific guidance is the available seed default; any site outside NSW requires a state-coverage review before reliance.
The design team must establish the building classification, applicable NCC edition, evidence pathway and authority sequence. The NCC pathway may combine Deemed-to-Satisfy documentation with Performance Solutions where the adopted fire, energy, accessibility, services or operational strategy cannot be demonstrated by DTS provisions. Each proposed Performance Solution requires its defined performance requirement, stakeholders, assessment method, evidence and certifier acceptance route before it affects programme or procurement. Do not allow discipline packages to make unrecorded changes to the accepted strategy.
A coordinated fire-safety basis is required before scheme lock. It is to identify classifications and interfaces, construction and compartmentation intent, egress and occupant assumptions, passive and active fire measures, emergency-service access, maintenance assumptions, authority consultation and inspection/commissioning gates. The Fire Engineer must coordinate a responsibility matrix allocating definition, design, installation, inspection, testing, certification and maintenance of each fire measure. Fire-services scope includes hydrant systems to AS 2419.1 and pumpsets to AS 2941; duty, storage, pump redundancy, water supply, brigade interface and commissioning witnesses remain **Assumption** until the fire strategy and hydraulic design are issued.
For a 15 MW tier-3 facility, electrical and mechanical compliance coordination must proceed with utility feasibility, resilience assumptions, plant-space planning, discharge/noise and environmental interfaces, control-system architecture and tested operational sequences. Security requirements are a derived risk flag, not a confirmed classification. The high-security warning does not establish a Top Secret/SCI or SCIF brief; obtain the owner security classification, workforce requirements and approving authority before specifying controlled construction measures. Similarly, the critical-infrastructure warning requires an applicability assessment and owner-led legal/security advice on SOCI Act obligations rather than an assumed statutory outcome.
The certifier, if appointed, should set a design-certification and inspection schedule. Required records are expected to include approved plans and conditions, consultant certifications, product evidence, test plans, commissioning scripts, essential safety measures documentation where applicable, fire-safety certificates, as-built records and occupation approval evidence. No Superintendent or Certifier role is implied by this PMP. Builder licence, insurances, applicable levy/long-service evidence and statutory instruments must be verified by the architect-PM before award and mobilisation.

## Cost Planning
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_1b1fea069100f517a324ad89d0afcfcd -->
The stated budget is $180m; its inclusions, escalation base date, GST treatment, contingency, consultant fees, authority charges, utility works, land costs, IT equipment, financing and operational readiness costs are **Assumption**. Establish a cost-plan basis before design selections or procurement commitments.
The cost plan should separately report site and shell works; electrical distribution and backup generation; cooling; controls/BMS; security; ICT interfaces; utility and vendor works; commissioning and integrated systems testing. This is a cost-control structure only, not a capacity design or market-rate benchmark. Reconcile every package against the agreed 15 MW and tier-3 brief, responsibility matrix and staged deployment assumption.
Maintain base estimate, risk allowance, design contingency, escalation allowance, provisional sums and client-held contingency as distinct lines. High-security and critical-infrastructure flags require allowance only after applicability and scope are confirmed. Recommendation: commission an independent cost plan and risk workshop before approval submission, then refresh it at design freeze, tender return, award and forecast completion. Variations require scope, programme, cost, risk and approval impact assessment before owner instruction.

## Procurement and Delivery
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_ad463b81a827ce0b6979a4175cd8c7f2 -->
No procurement route, builder list, contract form, tender criteria or delivery allocation is evidenced. Recommendation: complete the technical brief, approvals strategy, utility feasibility and consultant appointments before selecting route. A traditional construct-only route is the working assumption because it supports coordinated owner-controlled documentation and a competitive lump-sum tender; it must not be treated as an owner decision.
The architect-PM should prepare the procurement plan, package responsibility schedule, tender conditions, returnable schedule, clarification protocol, evaluation matrix and conflict declarations. Before issuing an RFT, establish the design maturity needed for reliable price comparison, identify owner-supplied/vendor equipment, set long-lead procurement controls and record the commissioning authority’s authority to witness integrated tests. Builder numbers are not specified; if traditional tender is retained, invite a suitably qualified shortlist only after security, capacity, experience, licences and insurances are assessed.
At award, verify the builder’s applicable licence, insurances, levy/long-service evidence, executed statutory instruments, programme, subcontractor controls, WHS arrangements, quality plan, test plans and security management plan. Contract administration must preserve notices, directions, variations, extensions of time, payment claims, completion and defects records. The project lead advises and coordinates; it does not assume contract administrator, Superintendent or certifier authority without appointment.
