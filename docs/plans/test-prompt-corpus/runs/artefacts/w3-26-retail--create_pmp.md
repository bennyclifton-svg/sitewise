## Brief
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_2b7645306cab39b653694cd455c93f3a -->
The physical brief is a new standalone Class 6 retail building of 3,200 m² GLA in NSW. The $7m figure is a stated budget, not a validated total-project-cost or construction-cost allowance. **Assumption** — the brief must establish site address, tenancy strategy, highway and utility interfaces, parking, access, servicing, landscape, signage, façade, structure, services, fire systems, fitout boundary and external works.
Acceptance criteria are an approved, documented and certifiable retail building; coordinated base-building/tenant interfaces; commissioned services; compliant access, egress and life-safety provisions; approved cost and programme baselines; and handover records. No physical exclusions are established. The owner must distinguish landlord/base-building works from tenant works before design development.

## Planning and Compliance
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_500c3effcc10b8d5b086694ea8ed9379 -->
**Assumption** — planning pathway is DA pending address, zoning, site constraints, development standards, traffic/parking and authority advice. The planning enquiry must test whether a CDC or another pathway is available; it must not be selected merely to accelerate delivery. A current consent, conditions, approved plans, certifier appointment and construction certificate evidence are not available.
NCC compliance documentation must identify, for each element, whether Deemed-to-Satisfy or Performance Solution is proposed. The design team must coordinate Class 6 classification, fire resistance and compartmentation, egress, accessibility, sanitary facilities, energy efficiency, façade/weatherproofing, structural design and services documentation with the certifier. Any Performance Solution requires its defined assessment process, stakeholder input and approval evidence before dependent documentation is released.
For this 3,200 m² retail format, planning due diligence must address the actual site context: permissible use, setbacks and built form, parking/loading, pedestrian and vehicle circulation, drainage, external lighting, waste, signage, road/highway access, utilities capacity and construction impacts. **Assumption** — flood, bushfire, heritage, contamination and ecology searches are required because no site results have been supplied. Do not infer a clear site from silence.
The fire-services brief must define hydrant, pump, detection/alarm, emergency lighting, exit signage and smoke-control requirements where applicable, then coordinate design, installation testing, commissioning and essential safety measure obligations. The certifier must identify inspection hold points and the final occupation pathway. The project lead will retain approval conditions, certificates, test records, warranties and ESM information in the handover record.

## Cost Planning
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_6548b43ee53eab440862a310af769ab4 -->
The stated $7m budget requires immediate definition of currency, GST treatment, escalation date, inclusions, exclusions and whether it is a construction-only or total-project budget. **Assumption** — it should be tested against a structured base-building cost plan rather than used as a pricing instruction. The cost planner should separate preliminaries, site establishment, demolition/site remediation if required, earthworks, structure, envelope, roof, internal base build, hydraulic, fire, mechanical, electrical, communications/security, external works, authority contributions, consultant fees, contingency and escalation. Tenant fitout must remain separately identified unless the owner directs otherwise.
No contingency, PC sums, provisional sums or benchmark basis is evidenced. Allowances should be risk-linked, transparent and progressively retired through surveys, consultant design and quotations; they are not a substitute for unresolved scope. Cost reports must reconcile approved budget, forecast, committed cost, variations, risks and cash flow at each Stage gate. The **recommendation** is to appoint/state an independent cost planner before scheme lock and require a cost-plan update before planning lodgement, before tender issue and before contract award.

## Procurement and Delivery
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_c3548b983da33f3493d4fad084cda41d -->
**Assumption** — traditional lump-sum procurement is the working route, subject to a documented owner decision after scope, risk allocation, programme and market input are tested. It suits a coordinated construct-only package, but only when design responsibility, approvals, base-building/tenant boundaries and specifications are sufficiently resolved. No builder count, tender list, pricing returnable, tender programme, evaluation criteria or conflict disclosure is evidenced; the project lead must not represent that any builder is prequalified or **invited**.
Before tender or direct negotiation, establish consultant deliverables, issued-document register, scope matrix, inclusions/exclusions, preliminaries, programme, quality requirements, statutory responsibilities, site logistics, services authority interfaces, warranties, commissioning, defects process and tender clarifications protocol. The owner should receive a comparative recommendation addressing price, qualifications, departures, programme, capability, financial checks and risk allocation. Contract form and amendments require legal review; no party is to be treated as superintendent, certifier or contract administrator without appointment evidence.
At mobilisation, verify the selected builder’s applicable licence, insurances, levy/long-service evidence, executed contract, construction approvals, WHS arrangements, programme, subcontractor coordination and statutory instruments before site start. Variations, extension-of-time claims, payment claims and notices must follow the executed contract and retain contemporaneous records.
