## Brief
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_83c754634725dc329d69600e8da4e5e3 -->
The physical brief is for a new commercial office building in NSW: Grade A, 12 storeys and 18,000 m² NLA. The $140m budget is a project-profile figure; its inclusions, escalation basis, contingency, consultant costs, authority charges and tax treatment require definition in the first cost plan.
No physical work-scope selections are established. **Assumption** — the base-building brief will define site preparation, substructure, structure, façade, roof, vertical transport, mechanical, electrical, hydraulic, fire, communications, security, public-domain works, landscaping, tenancy interfaces and commissioning requirements. The tenancy count, floor-plate strategy, parking, utility capacity and operational constraints are not evidenced.
Acceptance criteria are a coordinated approved design, certifier-ready compliance documentation, tender-ready scope allocation, tested and commissioned base-building systems, required occupation instruments, asset information and defects close-out. The architect-PM engagement brief is separate: it governs client-side advice, coordination, reporting and contract-administration support; it does not establish construction responsibility.

## Planning and Compliance
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_8613bcc15d5d435879ea524dead0b517 -->
The planning pathway is **Assumption** pending the site, land-use controls, height and floor-space tests, authority advice and owner development strategy. The pathway decision must be made from current statutory instruments and authority records; the current profile alone cannot establish DA, CDC, SSD or exempt development. For a 12-storey, 18,000 m² Grade A office proposal, the planning team must test the applicable environmental planning instrument, development control plan, design-excellence process, infrastructure and public-domain requirements, traffic and servicing impacts, heritage, flood, contamination, utilities and any State referral triggers.
The NCC compliance strategy is also **Assumption**. The project team must nominate, element by element, Deemed-to-Satisfy (DtS) or Performance Solution pathways. Certifier-ready documentation is expected to include coordinated architectural documentation; structural certification; fire-resistance, compartmentation, egress and fire-safety system design; accessibility; energy-efficiency documentation; services coordination; and evidence supporting each selected pathway. A Performance Solution requires a defined performance requirement, stakeholder consultation and an agreed assessment method before detailed design is locked.
Class 5 classification, mixed-use interfaces, effective height, rise in storeys, fire-source features, exit capacity, travel distances, accessible paths, sanitary facilities, façade performance, smoke control, lifts, plant access and energy provisions require early coordinated testing. **Assumption** — any retail, parking, end-of-trip, loading dock or plant levels will introduce additional classification and interface checks.
The certifier must be appointed before relying on a certification strategy. The fire engineer, services engineers and certifier must define inspection, test and witness points. Essential safety measures obligations, fire-safety schedules, commissioning records and occupation prerequisites must be identified from the approval and final design; none are evidenced. Fire-services scope is not selected, so no project-specific hydrant or pumpset standard can be nominated from the supplied material.

## Procurement and Delivery
<!-- clerk:block id=blk_54ec91d58eb4bdd7a2461385b186aa81 -->
Procurement route, pricing basis, builder count, contract form and tender timing are **Assumption**. Traditional construct-only procurement is the working route because it preserves a completed principal-led design and lump-sum tender comparison; it must not be treated as an approved project decision. Under that route, AS 4000 is a possible construct-only form subject to legal review and commercial amendments. A later Design & Construct route would require AS 4902 or a legally reviewed bespoke D&C form, not AS 4000.
Before market approach, appoint the design team, establish consultant deliverables, lock the approval strategy, reconcile the QS plan with the brief, allocate design responsibility, identify long-lead items and issue a coordinated tender package. The RFT should define scope boundaries, preliminaries, programme requirements, design-quality obligations, services commissioning, commissioning data, defects obligations, security, insurances, WHS, site constraints, tender clarifications and evaluation criteria. No builder number is stated because this is platform-seeded; when tender-ready, an invited field of 2–3 suitable builders may be considered following capability, capacity, financial and probity checks.
The architect-PM will advise the owner on tender process, comparative evaluation, programme and variation impacts. The owner remains the decision-maker unless executed instruments say otherwise. The builder’s applicable licence, insurances, levy/long-service evidence and statutory instruments must be verified before site mobilisation. No Superintendent or Certifier role is implied. Commercial fit-out-style landlord interfaces, fire/life-safety, accessibility, services capacity and occupation controls must be allocated even if later tenant works are separately procured.
