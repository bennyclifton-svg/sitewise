## Background

The project proposes multi-dwelling housing with Torrens subdivision across three lots totalling 3,240 m², with 12 dwellings identified as a feasibility target and attached Class 1a dwellings subject to certification. [8]
The hydraulic appointment is to support the project’s planning and delivery development while maintaining clear consultant responsibilities; existing reports or drawings do not by themselves verify an appointment. [8]
The current planning advice assumes a Council DA pathway, subject to confirmation of the site-specific statutory position before lodgement. [10]

## Services and deliverables

Provide a concise return brief with the tender response, identifying amendments, qualifications, omissions and additional services.

1. Review the development brief and available hydraulic drawings; establish the design basis for the proposed multi-dwelling housing and Torrens subdivision across three lots (3,240 m²), and identify investigations or information required before reliance on existing services or design assumptions. [8] 
2. Develop and coordinate hydraulic design documentation for the 12 attached Class 1a dwellings, including fixture scheduling, potable cold water, hot water, sanitary plumbing and drainage, sewer connections, gas and associated metering, isolation, access and maintainability requirements. [8] 
3. Coordinate below-ground drainage and site interfaces with the civil engineer, including stormwater connection and OSD interfaces; define the respective hydraulic/civil design, authority, construction-certification and handover responsibilities, noting that the civil proposal excludes detailed OSD design and construction certification. [11] 
4. Coordinate hydraulic layouts, schematics, plant and equipment locations, risers, penetrations and service zones with architectural, structural, civil, fire, mechanical, electrical, waterproofing and landscape documentation; resolve interfaces affecting wet areas, fire stopping, roof drainage and below-ground works. [4] 
5. Prepare stage-appropriate calculations, drawings, specifications, schedules and tender documentation; respond to tender queries and assess hydraulic substitutions or departures against the approved design intent. [6] 
6. Support the DA pathway by identifying the applicable hydraulic approval, authority and certification process, confirming the consultant’s pathway assumption where not evidenced, and preparing required submissions and responses for that process. [10] 
7. Provide construction-phase design support, including review of relevant shop drawings and RFIs, site inspections appropriate to the hydraulic role, and close-out through testing records, compliance documentation, as-built information, warranties and completion/certification records; exclude contractor installation, testing and statutory certification unless expressly appointed. [7]
